EXTRAsauce Source on GitHub

Your favourite looks, on your new camera.

Extra Sauce rebuilds camera look profiles for bodies they were never made for, so your old Lightroom presets keep working when you upgrade. It also turns phone-app presets into LUTs.

Camera profiles

One look, every camera

  1. Drop in your .dcp look profiles, or a whole folder of them.
  2. Tick the cameras you shoot with. There are 1,400+ to choose from.
  3. Click Convert. Each look is built for each camera and installed straight into Lightroom.

Converted profiles keep their original names, so your presets find them without any edits.

Phone presets

Rescue a preset you can't export

  1. Save the blank colour chart: 15,625 patches.
  2. Apply the preset to it on your phone and export it at full size.
  3. Drop it back in to get a 33-point .cube LUT.

Tested against an app's own export on a real photo, the LUT comes out at 1.0 ΔE on average, or about 1.7 ΔE once it's made into a Lightroom profile.

How it works

Every camera profile is a recipe Lightroom runs in a fixed order. The first two steps cancel out the quirks of one sensor. The last two are the style.

Look makers don't build each camera's profile from scratch. They start from Adobe Standard for that camera, which Adobe has tuned since 2008 so every camera's colour roughly agrees, and add their style on top. So if you have a look's profile for one camera and Adobe Standard for the same camera, the difference between them is the look itself, with the camera taken out. Extra Sauce works that difference out for every colour: it undoes Adobe Standard's calibration, then applies the look's own calibration and look table. It then fine-tunes the result against the original profile's actual output across 200,000 colours, which roughly halves what's left of the difference.

look = LookTable ∘ HueSatMap ∘ (Adobe Standard HueSatMap)⁻¹

To build the profile for your new camera, it starts from that camera's own Adobe Standard profile, whose matrices and calibration already handle the new sensor. It swaps in the extracted look, sampled across 90 hues, 25 saturations and 16 brightness levels, and copies across the original tone curve. The new file keeps the original profile name, so a preset that asks for that profile finds a version made for the new body.

It works inside the profile rather than as a filter on the finished image because by then the most saturated colours are already clipped, and no filter afterwards can bring them back. Inside the profile, the look sees the same colour data the original maker's did.

Each conversion checks itself by rebuilding the look for the camera it came from and comparing that with the real profile across 40,000 colours. If one look scores noticeably worse, try a different source camera: Adobe has revised some cameras' Adobe Standard profiles over the years.
